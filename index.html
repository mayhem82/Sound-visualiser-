My Journey Into Sight and Sound

— and code.

A personal suite of camera and microphone tools, built one feature at a time: turning sound into light, correcting colour for colourblind eyes, pushing that same correction further experimentally, matching paint to reality, recording performances, calling someone else entirely, counting flying foxes against a twilight sky, easing eye strain with real camera sensor control, and now, making sight and sound talk both ways.

The journey so far

It started with sound. Sound Nebula turned a microphone into light — bass, mids, and treble as their own swarms of colour, reacting in real time.

Then it became about colour, for a reason: Colour Assist was built for colourblind eyes — camera in hand, calibrating real colours by eye until they read right, instead of trusting a generic filter. Colour Vision Extreme took that same correction engine further, as an experimental space for pushing what it could do — cartoon mode, diagnosed colour-vision-deficiency correction, and whatever gets tried next. That same engine also powers Property Colour Reference, aimed at a different problem: matching real-estate photography to a known-true paint swatch.

From there, Video Production combined both threads: record a visual performance once, then trigger it live while filming, using the same correction and effects pipeline underneath.

Video Call took all of that (the camera pipeline, the device pairing, the styling) and pointed it at two people talking to each other, instead of one person and a mirror. No account, no server beyond a public relay for the first handshake. Just the same small toolkit, aimed at something new.

Flying Fox Count — the same edge-outline processing that makes Colour Assist and Colour Vision Extreme's outline mode work, pointed at a real problem instead of a demo: making bats stand out against a dusk or dawn sky, to help count a flying fox colony's emergence or return. Work in progress.

Blue Light Filter came next — a dedicated page for a warmer, less blue-heavy camera view in the evening. Rebuilt more than once along the way (a channel cut, then a real spectral transmission model) before settling on the honest version: no software recolouring at all, just exposing the camera's own real hardware sensors — colour temperature, exposure, focus — where the device actually reports them.

And the thread running through everything since that very first page — sound becoming light — finally grew a page of its own. See Sight ↔ Sound below.

Sight ↔ Sound

Sound has been turning into light here since the very first page, and Colour Vision Extreme's Audio colour tint already let bass, mids, and treble colour the corrected view live. Sound Colour made that connection run both ways — and it's kept growing since.

Sound → Colour, four ways now: bass/mid/treble boost the correction blend, spread, and outline strength directly (Audio → Correction); an audio-reactive particle swarm; Beat flash's torch pulse, screen flash, and vibrate; and Audio colour tint's original hue wash — each its own separate mechanism, not one shared control.

Colour → Sound, three ways: a melodic chime that plucks up a pentatonic scale as the camera nears a saved calibration colour (or, with no setup at all, plays live off how vivid whatever's currently on screen already is); the scene's dominant hue, either as a continuous drone or its own ambient arpeggio pad (a different scale, a different groove); and how busy/edgy the view is, either as a continuous filtered-noise texture or a percussive rhythm at a steady tempo, where busyness picks how many of 16 steps get struck each bar (a Euclidean pattern, not just a faster click) — and, for more than one bar, each bar rotates that same pattern by a fixed offset so a multi-bar loop actually progresses instead of repeating one identical bar forever.

Every melodic note can now play through more than this page's own built-in synth: MIDI out sends real notes to a connected instrument (hardware synth, or a virtual MIDI port feeding software on the same device) via the Web MIDI API, or Instrument plays real recorded General MIDI instrument samples fetched and decoded right in the browser — no bundled audio, no third-party library, just real sample files and the Web MIDI API doing exactly what they're for. Each of the three now picks from a genuinely large instrument library (112 pitched instruments — essentially the full General MIDI set, piano through ethnic — for the chime and dominant tone; 15 percussive and sound-effect ones for edge texture), with Range and Tempo controls tuned to what each one actually does, plus Detune (a real pitch shift, applied consistently across the synth, a MIDI pitch bend, or a resampled instrument) and Randomness (a touch of humanizing pitch jitter, plus real note-choice or rhythm-pattern variety, always off by default).

And each of the three now has a Pattern choice — not just a different-sounding output, but a genuinely different note/rhythm generator underneath, with the original design kept as the default: the chime can wander the scale in a closeness-biased random walk, or pluck a two-note third instead of one note; the dominant tone's arpeggio can wander the whole scale instead of a fixed chord shape, or drone on one repeated root note instead of cycling; and edge texture's rhythm can land on an evenly-spaced pulse instead of a syncopated Euclidean spread, keep that same spread but swing its 16th-note timing long-short instead of straight, or — the one Pattern here that isn't rhythm-only — keep Euclidean's placement but let pitch wander the scale (a Random walk) instead of density picking the note directly, the same note-choice idea chime's and dominant tone's own Random walk already use.

Dominant tone's Continuous drone can now play through MIDI/Instrument too, not just its own oscillator — genuinely, not as a one-shot substitute: over MIDI it holds a real note and pitch-bends it live, re-triggering only when it crosses a semitone (the default MIDI bend range can't cover a full octave on its own); over Instrument it loops one real sample and continuously re-tunes its playback speed, which has no semitone ceiling at all, so that one is properly, smoothly continuous.

Edge texture used to be pitch-less — every Melodic hit struck one fixed note, leaning entirely on the chosen instrument's own timbre. It has a real scale of its own now (a whole-tone scale, a genuinely different shape from the other two's pentatonic sound, rooted lower), and the same density driving how busy the rhythm gets also picks a pitch within it — so a hit is a real note, at every output, not just a fixed drum sound.

The newest piece: an opt-in Scientific colour mode, swapping hand-picked band hues for ones derived from real physics — each band's own measured frequency, folded up into the single octave visible light spans (~400–789 THz), read off as an actual wavelength and converted to a real colour, instead of one chosen for looks. That wavelength→colour step itself now runs on the real CIE 1931 colour-matching functions (the actual published dataset, not a fast piecewise approximation) — a compact analytic fit was tried first, verified against the real data, and rejected: it was excellent through most of the spectrum but drifted the deep-red tail's hue back through orange into green by 750nm, an artifact of its error growing relative to how small the real values get out there. The real dataset has no such artifact.

Also: Sound Nebula's beat-triggered Colour vision flash mode now crossfades between saved points instead of hard-cutting.

And now sight and sound reach past the screen entirely: DMX Lighting Control sends dominant colour, scene complexity, and bass/mid/treble/beat out over real DMX512, live, to actually drive the room's own LED bars, PAR lights, and a laser/strobe trigger — not a visual or audio effect this time, the physical lighting itself.

Chime, dominant tone, and edge texture now share one loudness ceiling instead of three different ones nobody had actually balanced against each other — chime used to read as noticeably louder than either, and edge texture's own Volume slider could barely be heard at all, because the scene-complexity signal driving it rarely reaches anywhere near its own calibrated maximum the way chime's closeness signal does; a curve now lifts that realistic middle of its range instead of only ever using the bottom of the dial. The Instrument library is no longer split by channel either — the 15 percussive/sound-effect instruments used to only be reachable from edge texture, and the 112 pitched ones only from chime/dominant tone; all three can now pick from either.

And dominant tone has an opt-in Scientific pitch mode now, covering both its styles — the reverse trip of Scientific colour above: instead of a hand-picked 220-440Hz range chosen for how it sounds, the hue's own real spectral wavelength gets converted back to its actual light frequency and folded down into that same octave, the same physics run backwards. Every colour really does get its own physically-derived note this way, not a range picked for comfort — with one honest exception: magenta/pink and the purples between violet and red aren't real spectral colours at all (human vision reads them as a mix of the spectrum's two ends, not one wavelength), so those clamp to whichever real spectral hue is actually closest instead of faking a wavelength that doesn't exist. Melodic's chord gets the same treatment: instead of a hue-quantized position on the equal-tempered pentatonic scale, the root is that same physically-derived frequency, and the chord around it is built from real just-intonation ratios — a real 3:2 perfect fifth, not equal temperament's 2^(7/12) compromise — landing exactly on root/third/fifth/octave, which the old equal-tempered version's own comment claimed but (built off a five-note scale with wraparound) never actually delivered.

Sound Colour can also record itself now — every other page here already could, this one couldn't. Record captures the exact composited view (correction, particle swarm, everything) as a real video file, with chime/dominant tone/edge texture's actual sonification mixed in as its audio track, not silent. Reachable from the HUD or, with the HUD tapped away or in fullscreen, the same floating Record button every other page in this suite already has — and recordings land in a real Recordings list (Video Production's own Takes, essentially), each with a preview, its channels used, and Download/Delete, instead of one instant download you'd better not have missed. The microphone can join the mix too now, its own separate permission and toggle -- and both it and the sonification get their own Recording volume slider, set before you actually start (or live, mid-recording) once you know the scene, so the balance between them is something you control rather than whatever the raw levels happen to be. And Cartoon mode is back — Colour Vision Extreme's flat colour bands and dark ink lines, duotone recolour presets included; this page trimmed its UI away early on to stay focused on Sight ↔ Sound, but the shader underneath never actually left, so it took a real toggle back, not a rebuild.

Dominant tone used to average the whole scene's RGB into one colour before it ever looked at hue — which throws away exactly the information that makes two scenes different: a frame split evenly between pure red and pure green averages to the same dull yellow-green as a frame that's that solid colour everywhere, so the two used to play the identical pitch. A real circular-statistics measure (the mean resultant length of every pixel's hue as a unit vector on the hue circle, weighted by chroma) now tells them apart, brightening or dulling the tone — genuinely, via a real sawtooth voice mixed in alongside the plain sine one, since a sine tone alone has no harmonics for a filter to actually shape — plus a resonance sweep on top, both reaching Synth, MIDI (real standard CC74 Brightness/CC71 Resonance), and Instrument alike, not just whichever output happened to ship first.

A fourth sonification channel now, genuinely different in kind from the other three: chime/dominant tone/edge texture each collapse the whole scene into a single number before it becomes sound — however many colour bands or filters ride on top of that number, none of them carry where something is in the frame. Spatial scan does, using the same core technique real sensory-substitution devices for blind users actually use (Peter Meijer's The vOICe): the scene sweeps left to right, and at any instant only the current column sounds, as a chord of fourteen voices (one per row, pitched high at the top of frame down to low at the bottom, spanning the real device's own published 500Hz–5000Hz range) loud where that row is bright and panned to the sweep's own left-right position. A full sweep is a literal soundscape of the scene's actual shape, not a statistic describing it — reachable through Synth, a real fourteen-note MIDI chord continuously modulated via genuine Polyphonic Key Pressure (not one-shot notes, which a ~50ms-per-column churn rate would make nonsense of), or Instrument, the same fourteen rows as real looped General MIDI samples.

DMX Lighting Control could only ever aim a fixture's colour, never its actual position — no fixture profile had a Pan or Tilt channel at all, so a moving head just sat there. It now computes the scene's own brightness-weighted centroid (where the light actually is, not just how much of it there is) and a real Pan/Tilt/Dimmer/RGB moving-head profile drives a fixture's physical aim straight off that, independent of whichever Source its colour channels use — sight steering an actual motor now, not just a colour. Also added a real RGBAW+UV profile for cheap 6-channel PARs, Amber approximated the same honest way White already was (the shared red/green amount, since true amber needs both), UV left at zero for any camera-driven fixture rather than fake a reading no camera's RGB sensor — or human vision — could ever actually register.

A new Colour variety Source for DMX, using the exact same real circular-statistics measure Sound Colour's dominant tone uses to tell two colour-cancelling scenes apart. Deliberately NOT used to "correct" the averaged Dominant colour Source's own RGB output the way it is in Sound Colour, though — a single RGB fixture really can only ever show one blended colour at once, and a scene split between opposing hues genuinely does mix down to grey on a real light exactly the same way it averages to grey here; that's correct physical colour mixing, not the information-loss bug a symbolic pitch choice has no such excuse for. So it's its own independent Source instead, free to drive a Dimmer, a Trigger, or a tinted effect off "how varied is the scene's colour right now" on any fixture, without pretending a single fixture could actually display that variety directly.

Pan/Tilt itself had a real gap of its own, the moment a rig has more than one moving head: it aimed every fixture at the same single whole-frame brightness centroid, which lands in the empty space between two separate bright spots (two spotlights on opposite walls, say) rather than on either one. It now finds every distinct bright region in the frame (the same flood-fill technique, verbatim, Sound Colour's own particle attractors already use for the identical reason), and each fixture gets a Region number: 0 tracks the single brightest spot, 1 the next-brightest, and so on — a second moving head set to Region 1 genuinely tracks a different spot instead of both converging on one point. Falls back to the old whole-frame centroid when a fixture's assigned region isn't currently detected (a dim scene, or fewer distinct spots than configured fixtures), rather than snapping to a stale or undefined position.

The suite

🌌

Sound Nebula

A live microphone visualiser — bass, mids, and treble each drive their own swarm of glowing particles around a pulsing core.

 👁️

Colour Vision Extreme

The experimental edge of the correction engine — pushing colour correction and photography techniques further, calibrated from colours you set yourself.

 🎨

Sound Colour

The correction engine turned into a two-way bridge between sight and sound — bass, mid, and treble reach into the live correction; the corrected view plays back as a melodic chime, a drone, and a texture.

 🎛️

DMX Lighting Control

Sight and sound reaching past the screen entirely — dominant colour, scene complexity, and bass/mid/treble/beat, live over real DMX512, driving actual LED bars, PAR lights, and a laser/strobe trigger in the room.

 🎯

Colour Assist

A colourblind assist app, work in progress — a stripped-down build of the same correction engine: calibration, outlines, and camera controls. Nothing here is proven yet.

 🏡

Property Colour Reference

Live camera colour correction for real-estate photography, matched to a known-true reference colour rather than a filter.

 🎬

Video Production

Record a visual performance once as a Template, then trigger it live while filming — Studio, Live, and Takes.

 📞

Video Call

Peer-to-peer video calling, direct between two devices — no account, no call server, every style transforms the image.

 🦇

Flying Fox Count

A counting aid for flying fox (bat) colony emergence and return, at sunset or sunrise — edge-outline highlighting plus a manual tally. Work in progress, not a validated counter.

 🌙

Blue Light Filter

Real camera-hardware sensor control for evening viewing — colour temperature, exposure, and focus, adjusted at the sensor itself where the device exposes them. The view always stays true; no software recolour.

 📺

Tablet Viewer

A read-only viewer for watching any of the above pages' live broadcasts on a second device, like a tablet or TV.

 🎓

Video Tutorials

Short how-to clips — getting started, fine-tuning a colour, colour blindness type and spread, two-device setup.
