My Journey Into Sight and Sound

— and code.

A personal suite of camera and microphone tools, built one feature at a time: turning sound into light, correcting colour for colourblind eyes, pushing that same correction further experimentally, matching paint to reality, recording performances, calling someone else entirely, counting flying foxes against a twilight sky, easing eye strain with real camera sensor control, and now, making sight and sound talk both ways.

The journey so far

It started with sound: Sound Nebula turned a microphone into light. Then colour, for a reason — Colour Assist was built for colourblind eyes, calibrated from real colours rather than a generic filter; Colour Vision Extreme pushed that same correction engine further as an experimental space; Property Colour Reference repointed it at matching real-estate photography to a known-true paint swatch. Video Production added recording a performance and triggering it live; Video Call pointed the whole pipeline at two people talking instead of one person and a mirror; Flying Fox Count repurposed the same edge-outline technique to help count a bat colony at dusk; Blue Light Filter settled on real camera-sensor control, no software recolour.

Sound Colour made sight and sound run both ways — bass/mid/treble drive the live correction, a particle swarm, and flash/vibrate; a calibrated colour plays back as a melodic chime, a drone, a rhythmic texture, or (sweeping the whole frame into a real sensory-substitution soundscape) a spatial scan, through this page's own synth, real MIDI, or real instrument samples, with optional modes that derive pitch and colour from actual physics rather than a look chosen by ear or eye. It records itself too, sonification and mic mixed into a real video file.

DMX Lighting Control sends sight and sound past the screen entirely, out over real DMX512 to actual lights — colour, complexity, colour variety, audio bands/beat, screen capture, and the device's own motion sensors as live inputs; moving heads get real Pan/Tilt tracking distinct bright regions; fixture setups save and load as named rigs. Colour Alarm sounds a genuine siren the moment a calibrated colour enters frame. Camera Hub gives every feature one shared camera session instead of a fresh permission prompt each time.

A run of features since has leaned on real device/browser capabilities most apps never touch: real time-lapse, a live histogram, the system eyedropper, native save/open dialogs and share sheet, frame-accurate correction of an existing animated GIF/WebP, and Blue Light Filter's pass-through suppression timed off actual sunrise/sunset at your real location.

Most recently, a real Shape Detection round trip spread across the whole suite — scanning a rig, a colour code, a room code, or a saved-item list back in wherever copying one out already existed — and, since that always used to end with "paste it into any free QR generator," a genuine from-scratch QR encoder (real Reed-Solomon error correction, no library) now renders the code directly in the page too.

The suite

🌌

Sound Nebula

A live microphone visualiser — bass, mids, and treble each drive their own swarm of glowing particles around a pulsing core.

 👁️

Colour Vision Extreme

The experimental edge of the correction engine — pushing colour correction and photography techniques further, calibrated from colours you set yourself.

 🎨

Sound Colour

The correction engine turned into a two-way bridge between sight and sound — bass, mid, and treble reach into the live correction; the corrected view plays back as a melodic chime, a drone, and a texture.

 🎛️

DMX Lighting Control

Sight and sound reaching past the screen entirely — dominant colour, scene complexity, and bass/mid/treble/beat, live over real DMX512, driving actual LED bars, PAR lights, and a laser/strobe trigger in the room.

 🚨

Colour Alarm

Calibrate a real colour — a hi-vis vest, a specific shirt — and a real audible siren sounds the moment it appears anywhere in the camera's view, not just at a fixed aim point. Built to notice something entering frame, not to identify what's already there.

 🎯

Colour Assist

A colourblind assist app, work in progress — a stripped-down build of the same correction engine: calibration, outlines, and camera controls. Nothing here is proven yet.

 🏡

Property Colour Reference

Live camera colour correction for real-estate photography, matched to a known-true reference colour rather than a filter.

 🎬

Video Production

Record a visual performance once as a Template, then trigger it live while filming — Studio, Live, and Takes.

 📞

Video Call

Peer-to-peer video calling, direct between two devices — no account, no call server, every style transforms the image.

 🦇

Flying Fox Count

A counting aid for flying fox (bat) colony emergence and return, at sunset or sunrise — edge-outline highlighting plus a manual tally. Work in progress, not a validated counter.

 🌙

Blue Light Filter

Real camera-hardware sensor control for evening viewing — colour temperature, exposure, and focus, adjusted at the sensor itself where the device exposes them. The view always stays true; no software recolour.

 🧭

Camera Hub

One camera session, every feature one tap away -- no separate page load or repeated permission prompt to switch between them. Blue Light Filter is live; the rest of the suite is being wired in one feature at a time.

 🔌

Sensor Bridge

Reads (and sends to) an ESP32/Arduino-class board over the Web Serial API -- the same connection this suite's DMX page already uses, generalised to plain text/JSON sensor lines instead of one fixed protocol.

 📳

Vibration Scan

Pulses the phone's own vibration motor and reads the accelerometer's response, comparing points as you sweep along a pipe or panel. A comparative aid only -- not a validated crack or defect detector.

 📺

Tablet Viewer

A read-only viewer for watching any of the above pages' live broadcasts on a second device, like a tablet or TV.

 🎓

Video Tutorials

Short how-to clips — getting started, fine-tuning a colour, colour blindness type and spread, two-device setup.
