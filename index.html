My Journey Into Sight and Sound

— and code.

A personal suite of camera and microphone tools, built one feature at a time: correcting colour for colourblind eyes, turning sound into light, matching paint to reality, recording performances, and now, calling someone else entirely.

The journey so far

It started with colour. Colour Vision Extreme began as a way to correct the world back into focus — camera in hand, calibrating real colours by eye until they read right, instead of trusting a generic filter. That same correction engine went on to power Colour Assist (the focused, everyday version) and Property Colour Reference (matching real-estate photography to a known-true paint swatch).

Then it became about sound. Sound Nebula turned a microphone into light — bass, mids, and treble as their own swarms of colour, reacting in real time. From there, Video Production combined both threads: record a visual performance once, then trigger it live while filming, using the same correction and effects pipeline underneath.

The newest piece is Video Call — taking all of that (the camera pipeline, the device pairing, the styling) and pointing it at two people talking to each other, instead of one person and a mirror. No account, no server beyond a public relay for the first handshake. Just the same small toolkit, aimed at something new.
