Theremin

Two ways to play, picked below once open: Doppler plays a fixed near-ultrasonic tone through the speaker and reads its echo with the microphone -- a moving hand nearby shifts that echo's frequency, driving pitch/volume, a one-antenna analogue of a real theremin's proximity sensing. Tilt + Touch instead uses two real, independent inputs like a real theremin's two antennas -- tilt the phone left/right for pitch, touch the pad with a finger for volume (no touch at all is silence, same as a real theremin's hand-away-from-the-loop silence).

Doppler is an approximation, not a distance measurement -- "something moved closer/further," never an exact distance, and depends heavily on your device's speaker/mic and a quiet room; the probe tone sits at the edge of human hearing and may be faintly audible. Tilt + Touch are real, direct readings (no approximation), just a different physical interaction -- you're moving the device and touching a pad, not sensing a hand near a still one.

Doppler: proximity/motion analogue only, not a ranging tool. The pitch you hear tracks a smoothed estimate of "hand approaching vs. receding," not a real distance. Loud rooms, other ultrasonic-capable devices, and quiet speakers/mics all degrade it -- if nothing seems to respond, try moving your hand within about 20–30cm of the phone in a quiet room.

Tilt + Touch: real, direct readings, no approximation -- but it's the phone's own tilt and a touch on the pad, not a hand's distance in mid-air. Unlike Doppler, holding a tilt angle holds the pitch (nothing decays back to centre on its own), and volume is silent until you actually touch the pad.
