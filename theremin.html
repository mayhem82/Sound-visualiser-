Theremin

Two ways to play, picked below once open: Doppler plays a fixed near-ultrasonic tone through the speaker and reads its echo with the microphone -- a moving hand nearby shifts that echo's frequency, driving pitch/volume, a one-antenna analogue of a real theremin's proximity sensing. Tilt + Touch instead uses three real, independent, simultaneous inputs from the phone's own sensors -- tilt left/right (gamma) for pitch, tilt forward/back (beta) for a real echo/delay effect, and touch the pad with a finger for volume (no touch at all is silence, same as a real theremin's hand-away-from-the-loop silence). All three can move at once, since they're genuinely separate axes, not one axis reused for two jobs.

Doppler is an approximation, not a distance measurement -- "something moved closer/further," never an exact distance, and depends heavily on your device's speaker/mic and a quiet room; the probe tone sits at the edge of human hearing and may be faintly audible. Tilt + Touch are real, direct readings (no approximation), just a different physical interaction -- you're moving the device and touching a pad, not sensing a hand near a still one.

Probe tone: off Microphone: off Orientation sensor: off

Doppler: proximity/motion analogue only, not a ranging tool. The pitch you hear tracks a smoothed estimate of "hand approaching vs. receding," not a real distance. Loud rooms, other ultrasonic-capable devices, and quiet speakers/mics all degrade it -- if nothing seems to respond, try moving your hand within about 20–30cm of the phone in a quiet room.

Tilt + Touch: real, direct readings, no approximation -- but it's the phone's own tilt and a touch on the pad, not a hand's distance in mid-air. Left/right tilt (gamma) is pitch, holding an angle holds the pitch (unlike Doppler's decay-to-centre); forward/back tilt (beta), either direction, independently drives a real echo/delay effect -- both axes work at the same time, so tilting right AND forward at once means a higher pitch with more echo, not one replacing the other. MIDI output can't carry the echo effect (nothing local to process on a remote device's own note) -- only Synth and Instrument get it.
